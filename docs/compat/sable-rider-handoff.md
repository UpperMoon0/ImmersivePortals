# Detached rider prediction during Sable handoff

Main CI [38159514148](https://github.com/UpperMoon0/ImmersivePortals/actions/runs/38159514148), UDP job 114529327652, exposed a client prediction defect during the ordered seat-detach/dimension/Ack packet window. The captured source body stayed at y=-62.87 while the detached local player fell to y=-80.77 after 20 client ticks and y=-96.51 after 30. The destination body was already staged at y=133.1; no dimension transition had been applied. The existing 32-block spatial-continuity assertion correctly failed. TCP passed in that run. This was not an installation or runner-startup failure.

The compatibility layer now suspends only LocalPlayer.aiStep (local input/movement prediction) for the captured detached rider, in the correlated source/destination dimensions. The client and network continue ticking; authoritative packets remain free to apply position and dimension changes. It does not overwrite position, velocity, gravity attributes, flight flags, or unrelated players. Mounted players and unrelated coordinate frames are not held.

The hold is bounded by the existing 100-tick rider-sync constant and is released on attachment, rejection, cancellation, client cleanup/disconnect, or expiry. A replacement player instance, an unrelated dimension, or an unrelated seat permanently invalidates the handoff and releases portal gating; returning to the original frame cannot rearm it. Reattachment completes movement suspension, so a later dismount remains ordinary on-foot movement. The protocol lifetime and invalidation observer continue until Ack, cancellation, or expiry, so a missing Ack cannot strand portal gating. The existing post-Ack seat timer and fallback camera transform remain in place. The E2E 120-tick correlated-detach budget, 32-block distance limit, overlap budget, and three-transition assertions are unchanged.

## Regression

Before requesting the first real crossing, the dedicated client uses its actual captured seat and dimension-stack portal to reproduce a pending handoff locally. The server keeps the source body stationary until its normal source-ready marker. The client detaches, executes 40 ordinary client ticks without an Ack, and requires its world position to remain within 0.01 blocks. It then remounts the original seat without acknowledging the probe, verifies movement suspension ends while the handoff remains pending, and requires that pending state to clear at the unchanged 100-tick deadline. This catches losing the protocol watchdog when movement completes. It then runs every existing real handoff/gravity/dismount/remote-observer check. No sleeps or extended budgets are used. Both transport lanes require the new authoritative client-movement-hold marker.

JUnit separately checks player-instance identity, terminal source→third→source invalidation, reattachment/dismount non-rearming, unrelated-seat invalidation, the 100-tick expiry, immediate cancellation, and a delayed-Ack prediction model. Native runtime verification is required before merging; local Python or standalone Java compilation is not a substitute.

The native client additionally calls the production lifecycle entry points for rejected and stale/duplicate Acks, explicit cancellation, a replacement LocalPlayer with the same UUID, death, an unrelated seat, a source→third→source dimension sequence, and remount/dismount non-rearming. These are isolated lifecycle injections into real Minecraft objects; they do not claim to reproduce server respawn or unrelated teleport packet delivery. A second timed probe verifies the original post-Ack passenger timeout/fallback. At the end of the real crossings, the client posts the production cleanup event, rearms a handoff, and actually disconnects; pending state and movement suspension must both clear. Every native lane requires distinct lifecycle, post-Ack-timeout and disconnect markers.

Each native lane also repeats 20 ten-tick detach/reject/remount cycles, delivering the previous cycle's stale Ack while the new handoff is pending. The captured player must remain stationary, the stale Ack must not consume newer state, and each terminal rejection must release movement and pending state. A movement-soak marker is mandatory. This is a bounded repetition regression, not an hours-long multiplayer soak.

CI runs both transports with owo-lib's bundled networking API, TxniLib's 4.3.0+5c124ecf19, and 4.3.1+7e3d421f19. Two additional 4.3.1 lanes use Linux netem with 30 ms delay, 10 ms jitter, 0.5% packet loss, and a fixed seed. Filters restrict impairment to the selected Minecraft/Sable port. Nonzero traffic through the impairment queue is required and recorded in network-stress.json; the queue is removed even on setup/test failure. These are bounded CI scenarios, not a hardware/driver certification matrix.

This patch does not add scaled Sable structures or lift the scaling-portal traversal guard. Issue #24's moving-miniature milestone remains unfinished.
